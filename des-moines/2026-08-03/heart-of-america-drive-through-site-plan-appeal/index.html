      Des Moines › Aug 3, 2026 meeting › Item 60

      The council approved design exceptions for a drive-through restaurant at 100 SW 11th Street, overturning the Plan and Zoning Commission, by a 6–1 vote.

      Des Moines City Council · Aug 3, 2026 · Roll call 26-0930

      Approved the 10 requested design exceptions (Alternative B), with findings that the lot’s shape and isolation make typical downtown designs challenging, and added them to the approved site plan. Moved by Simonson, seconded by Gatto. Passed 6–1; voting no: Rob X. Barron.

      What this means (Rezoning): Zoning sets what can be built or done on each piece of land, such as homes, shops or industry.

      More from the record

      Heart of America (developer, represented by an officer of the company, who told the commission he owns Hyper Energy Bar) asked to build a drive-through focused restaurant with car and bike parking, patios and shade structures on a vacant 2.1-acre lot at 100 SW 11th Street, next to Martin Luther King Jr. Parkway. The lot is remnant land owned by the City from building the parkway, and is zoned DX2 Downtown. The plan needs 10 exceptions to downtown design rules, including a 1-story building where 3 stories are required, a 140-foot setback where 0–5 feet is required, surface parking and a drive-through, both not allowed in DX2. City staff recommended approval. On June 18, 2026 the Plan and Zoning Commission’s motion to approve failed 5–5, with one member passing, and the developer appealed to the council. Resolution and P&Z report, roll call 26-0930 (saved copy)

      The alternative

      The resolution listed one other option, Alternative A: deny the design exceptions, which would have left the commission’s outcome in place and the lot undeveloped under this plan. One commissioner suggested at least removing the drive-through. Resolution 26-0930, Alternative A and P&Z minutes (saved copy)

      Time and getting around

      Who
      Drivers, cyclists and people walking along the parkway
      How
      Drive-through (two lanes, per commission discussion) and surface parking. At the commission hearing, the city’s planning administrator said a sidewalk and bike trail would be added along the north side of the parkway, extending to about SW 14th Street. The developer said its traffic study showed cars should not stack up or block traffic at peak times; a speaker said the traffic study was not finalized and raised concerns about cars crossing bike lanes (claims made at the hearing).
