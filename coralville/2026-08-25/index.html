      • Vote 21. Motion on proposal to censure Mayor Laurie Goodrich. <Moved to follow community comments.> Councilperson Hai Huynh arrived at 6:35 PM. Community Comments: Alannah Swenka stated the most important thing a public servant can do at a bare minimum is let its citizens talk and it should not be lim Motion: that the City Council of the City of Coralville, Iowa to formally censure Mayor Laurie Goodrich. The City Council has a responsibility to uphold the Constitution of the United States and the State of Iowa and to protect the rights of residents to participate in their local government. Criticism of government and public officials lie at the core of speech protected by the 1st Amendment, and residents are entitled to express their view forcefully, subject only to lawful viewpoint neutral rules governing time order and genuine disruption. This censure is based on the following actions. One, conduct at the July 28th Council Meeting. Mayor Goodrich responded by describing their remarks as horrible, mean, cruel and unkind. to preserve the record a video of this exchange will be shown. Goodrich noted council members have requested that a video be shown. Goodrich stated she is ashamed of what the video depicts and recognizes that she should have remained silent. It was after the meeting. The video of the interaction of the mayor and residents after the July 28th Council Meeting was then played in the Council Chambers and can be viewed on the city website, www.coralville.org. (Go to the Agenda and Minutes webpage and play the video recording of the August 25, 2026 Coralville City Council Regular Meeting by clicking on the video recording link that looks like a television in a box. This action departed from Coralville’s established practice for agenda setting and public participation and could reasonably be interpreted as retaliatory towards residents who have spoken at the prior meeting. In support of public censure, the Council affirms that residents have the right to criticize the government and it’s officials even in language that elected officials might regard as harsh, unfair, unkind or unwelcome. The right to speak cannot depend on tone or whether elected official approve of the message. The Council does not ratify the mayor’s conduct and clearly distinguishes the city’s position from action that threatens residents’ constitutional rights. Councilperson Mike Knudson stated he worked with his fellow council members to draft the statement and agrees with what Councilperson Hai Huynh read. Councilperson Rich Vogelzang quoted a statement on his desk that has been a part of his business conduct for as long as he can remember, “The most dangerous form of blindness is believing that your perspective is the only reality.” Vogelzang thought that applies to a lot of aspects in business and personal life and in these chambers as well. All elected persons, whether local, regional, state or federal are expected to be held to a higher standard, but they are also human and Huynh on a number of occasions we do have personal lives in addition to our public lives and we’re all human. Every single human has a limit to their tolerance. There can be no denying that. The limits on tolerance can change depending on different circumstances whether you are taking a personal hit or whether you are trying to protect a family member or someone you respect. The circumstances can change that level of tolerance. Goodrich is a good person. She’s had many years of public service in this community, but everyone makes mistakes and has missteps. They make errors in their lives and Goodrich has acknowledged that. Goodrich stated again she takes full responsibility for her actions and will not make that mistake again. Councilperson Royce Peterson stated the letter that was put together pretty much sums it up. It was written well. This is very hard and Peterson thinks we all care about our community. Everyone of us, every single person here obviously or they wouldn’t be here. We take this very seriously and it’s just really tough to go through this all the way around. Peterson agreed 100% with the comments tonight and that is all he has to say. Huynh read the following:” In a democratic government residents have a fundamental right to engage elected official. That right is not only protected, it is encouraged. Trust in government is built when leaders remain collaborative, accessible and committed to working together for the greater good of the community. We must put our community first and our personal feelings last. Our responsibility is to stay unbiased, remain neutral in our conduct, listen openly and approach every situation with a solution- seeking mindset. Listening to community members is one of the most important responsibility we hold. Their voices guide our decisions and remind us who we serve, even when feedback is difficult to hear. Our job is to remain professional, neutral, and open to all perspectives. The ripple effect of our action when we act hastily not only fractured trust but can potentially cause harm to the very people we have sworn to serve and protect. Accountability means having the courage to own up to our own mistakes and acknowledge them to our constituents with a sincere apology, followed by action that improve the way we serve our community. We work for the people. The power vested in us come from the people, and it can be taken away by the people. When we swear an oath to serve our duty is to the office that residents elected us to. Not to our personal preferences or viewpoints. I believe in learning on the job, and I believe that elected official can grow from challenging experiences. Therefore, I will not call for Mayor Goodrich resignation, after what has occurred and the action taken by this council. I have confidence that our governing body can move forward together and continue serving Coralville with integrity even when our personal point of view differ. As leaders we must conduct ourselves with the utmost professionalism. We must listen to all perspectives, remain neutral, and continually work to maintain and improve our processes and our procedures. I hope this is a valuable lesson for each of us here today to learn from as we look ahead to serving our community better and strengthening the trust our residents place in us. Thank you.” Councilperson Katie Freeman thanked all their colleagues for being willing to talk and come to a conclusion that we need to support the rights of our constituents, our residents to speak with us through all avenues available to them. Freeman hopes that they can move forward from this moment and really focus on things to continue to make the community a better place.
