Resolution #021026-05 – Accept Public Improvements in Hawthorn Townhomes Plats 1 & 2 and Authorize Release of Performance Bond Murray/ Motion: to approve Resolution #021026-05 – Accept Public Improvements in Hawthorn Townhomes Plats 1 & 2 and Authorize Release of Performance Bond. Assistant City Manager/Community Development Director Madeline Sturms explained Hawthorn Townhomes are generally located between Martha Miller Drive and Meacham Drive, and it is directly east of the Forge65 Apartments. The final plat was originally submitted to City Council for review and approval on July 11, 2023, via Resolution 071123-02. At that time, the developer requested the final plat be considered prior to the completion of all public improvements to facilitate a transaction of the property and to begin construction of the townhomes and internal site improvements. In accordance with guidance set by City Code Chapter 175: Subdivision Regulations, a performance bond in the estimated amount of the proposed public improvements (sanitary sewer and water mains) was submitted to guarantee their installation or cover the cost of completing the public improvements. Hawthorn Townhomes Plat 2 was then submitted to City Council for review and approval on May 14, 2024, via Resolution 051424-07, which included minor adjustments to the lot widths. At this time, construction of all public improvements required by the plat and the performance bond have been completed and have been accepted by the City. The performance bond may be released after Council approval of the resolution. The City has also received the required maintenance bonds for the new sanitary sewer main and all appurtenances within the subdivision. Although the City is in receipt of the maintenance bond, the term of the bond shall begin on the date of formal acceptance of the public improvements and remain in effect for a period of four yearsPassed 4–0 · Minutes · Full page →

      • Vote 11i. Resolution #021026-12 – Approval of Professional Services Agreement with Axiom Consultants for Public Works and Parks Site Selection Murray/ Motion: to approve Resolution #021026-12 – Approval of Professional Services Agreement with Axiom Consultants for Public Works and Parks Site Selection. Assistant City Manager Madeline Sturms explained the City of Pleasant Hill has prioritized the relocation of the existing Public Works and Parks Facilities in the adopted Comprehensive Plan and Strategic Priorities previously considered on the Council agenda. The current facilities are impacted by a variety of concerns including flooding, access and security, and an inability to protect equipment and vehicles from the elements. To provide high-quality community services, facilities and infrastructure for future growth (Goal 9.1 of the Pleasant Hill 2050 Comprehensive Plan) a new shared facility should be constructed for efficiency and quality of service. The professional services agreement with Axiom Consultants is for the site selection process to determine the appropriate location for the facility that serves the long-term needs of the community. Axiom is a well-qualified firm that will be able to bring fresh perspective and expertise to the effort. The firm operates in the Des Moines market and has a strong history in the work the City is looking for. The consultant will engage with an architect to determine building massing, understand any adjacencies that may come with a specific site, and provide findings on a select number of sites that are recommended for the building location. The process will include cost estimating, site plan concept development, and continue through to assist the City with architect selection for the next phase of the project. A representative from Axiom Consultants, briefly spoke about his company and the services they offer, as well as answer any questionsPassed 5–0 · Minutes · Full page →
        What this means

        Site plan: A detailed drawing of how one property will be built, such as buildings, parking, driveways and landscaping. The city checks it against its rules.

      • Vote 13k. Resolution #021026-14 – Approval of Professional Services Agreement with Bolton and Menk Murray/ Motion: to approve Resolution #021026-14 – Approval of Professional Services Agreement with Bolton and Menk. Assistant City Manager/Community Development Director Madeline Sturms explained that the City of Pleasant Hill has a capital improvement program that includes plans for the future reconstruction of NE 70th Street in an area between University Avenue and the corporate boundary with the City of Altoona. The plans include the urbanization and widening of the roadway, realignment of the intersection with NE 12th Avenue, the incorporation of pedestrian/multimodal pathways, street lighting, and other safety and utility work. The City currently has the roundabout for an intersection with the upcoming Elevation Drive under final design with bidding and construction planned for the 2027 construction season. The City of Altoona has coordinating plans for the reconstruction of the same roadway (known as 1st Avenue South) from the corporate limits to 36th Street (County NE 27th Avenue). Altoona has previously submitted an application to the US Department of Transportation’s Better Utilizing Investments to Leverage Development (BUILD) grant program and is planning to submit the project again this round. Pleasant Hill has proposed a joint application for both projects to highlight regional cooperation and a larger public benefit. Staff has coordinated this possibility with Altoona staff and the consultant working for Altoona resulting in the scope of services with Bolton and Menk. The firm would coordinate and incorporate the Pleasant Hill work into a joint application. The resolution approves a professional services agreement with Bolton and Menk. Madison Burke, a representative from Bolton and Menk, was present at the meeting to speak about the project and potential grant funding, as well as answer questions or address concernsPassed 5–0 · Minutes
        What this means

        Capital improvement plan: A multi-year list of planned big projects, such as streets, buildings and equipment, with estimated costs. Projects on it still need their own approvals.

      • Vote 15m. Third Reading of Ordinance #966 – Amending Chapter 115: Definitions, Rights of Interment Certificate Holder(s), Rules for Visitors, Monuments, Plantings and Decorations Murray/ Motion: to approve the Third Reading of Ordinance #966 – Amending Chapter 115: Definitions, Rights of Interment Certificate Holder(s), Rules for Visitors, Monuments, Plantings and Decorations. Parks and Recreation Manager Ryan Merritt explained the proposed updates to Chapter 115 (Cemetery) focus on improving clarity, consistency, and safety while preserving respectful memorial practices at Oakwood Cemetery. Key changes include clearer standards for on‑memorial decorations and floral arrangements; a fourteen (14) day grace period following burials for funeral and sympathy items; updated flag allowances consistent with current federal guidance; refined placement zones and definitions; and improved rules for lighting and prohibited items. Cleanup periods have been standardized to three annual windows to reduce disruption and avoid major holidays. New notice, storage, and appeal procedures are also included to improve transparency and communication with families. The Parks and Recreation Commission/Cemetery Committee reviewed these proposed updates at its Thursday, December 11 meeting. The proposed changes were also shared with residents in attendance at the November 13 Parks and Recreation Commission meeting. The Commission formally supported the updates and recommended Council review. There have been no comments or questions since the previous readingsPassed 5–0 · Minutes
        What this means

        Ordinance readings: An ordinance is a local law. In Iowa it is usually voted on at three separate meetings (first, second and third reading) before it passes. Each reading is one of those votes.
