      • Vote 4b. Resolution #102726-08 – Order Construction of Public Sidewalks on Outlots in Pleasant Ridge Estates Plat 2 Birch/ Motion: to approve Resolution #102726-08 – Order Construction of Public Sidewalks on Outlots in Pleasant Ridge Estates Plat 2 City Manager Ben Champ explained the item. The Pleasant Ridge Estates neighborhood, on the east side of South Shadyview Boulevard and east of the bypass includes a small neighborhood that was originally developed in 2005. In the middle of the development there was intended to be an HOA property, however, the development went through a series of challenges during the housing crisis. Some parcels are not locally owned and have gone to tax sale. The City is now at a point where the majority of lots have been developed and there is interest from the neighborhood in completing the sidewalk gaps, specifically on the outlots. Notice has been provided to the owners of the outlots, but have not received response regarding sidewalk installation. The next step would be for Council to order installation of the sidewalks and assess the associated costs to the parcels. This work would be included in the next concrete patchwork project. Mr. Champ noted lots that don’t yet have houses would not have sidewalks completed as those sidewalks could be damaged during the construction and would subsequently need replacement. Councilmember DeMoss wanted to ensure clarity of what expectations were when the properties were purchased. Mr. Champ explained that since these were outlots, they had sidewalk locations known and described on platting documents that were approved. Regarding the middle parcel, it is owned by a group in Council Bluffs that purchased it through a tax sale. City staff cannot confirm if that group did research or reviewed publicly available documents, however, they have not been responsive to the City’s communication. DeMoss then asked what the cost of the assessed sidewalk would be. Mr. Champ noted there are about 400 linear feet of sidewalk needed, however, the City would not know the cost until the project is bid out. He also noted that the Council would not have to do an assessment for the costs, however, it is available in this instancePassed 5–0 · Minutes, p. 1 · Full page →

      • Vote 5c. Resolution #102726-09 – Approve 1st Amendment to the Economic Development and Infrastructure Agreement with LRE IA Pleasant Hill, LLC DeMoss/ Motion: to approve Resolution #102726-09 – Approve 1st Amendment to the Economic Development and Infrastructure Agreement with LRE IA Pleasant Hill, LLC. City Manager Ben Champ, provided the background on the existing agreement which was approved September 24, 2024. During platting for the site, there were infrastructure improvements required that result in the 1st Amendment to the Agreement being considered that clarify responsibility of the stormwater improvements which are located in the Iowa Department of Transportation (DOT) right-of-way. The DOT does not allow private infrastructure in their right- of-way which requires the infrastructure to be public and the responsibility of the City, the agreement allows the City to maintain and invoice the developer for all costs associated with the maintenance of the infrastructure. The developer also entered into a minimum assessment agreement that states that the minimum assessed value of the property be not less than $2,000,000. The amended agreement provides a new timeline to reach this minimum assessment to be January 1, 2028. Councilmember Hodges inquired if the City’s work being performed on University Avenue is what triggered the process of finding new providers since providers were having issues. Mr. Champ noted that the developers delay is not related to the City’s infrastructure project, while it did take longer that the City would have liked for it to occur, the developer originally had a user working very quickly but eventually had a situation which caused the developer to look for other users. While the developer has started working on the project site, the City does not have any site plans. Councilmember DeMoss asked if the developer wants the City to be an intermediary. Mr. Champ confirmed the City would be an intermediary and responsible party for the right-of-way as the Iowa Department of Transportation requires it. Due to the large size of the right-of-way, portions of the stormwater infrastructure will need to be in the right-of-way. To allow the infrastructure to be in the right-of-way, the City needs to be the owner, however, to not burden the taxpayer so costs would be recoverable and assigned back to the developerPassed 5–0 · $2,000,000 · Minutes, p. 2 · Full page →

        What this means: Site plan. A detailed drawing of how one property will be built, such as buildings, parking, driveways and landscaping. The city checks it against its rules.
