      • Vote 4a. Third Reading of Ordinance #969 - An Ordinance Amending Chapter 98 Sewer Service Charges Hodges/ Motion: to approve Third Reading of Ordinance #969 - An Ordinance Amending Chapter 98 Sewer Service Charges. City Manager Ben Champ explained that during the preparation of the FY27 budget it was identified that the rates for sanitary sewer service needed to be increased. The City of Pleasant Hill is a member of the Wastewater Reclamation Authority (WRA) and as a member must pay annually to the WRA for the City’s proportionate share of operation and maintenance costs and for debt service payments. The WRA has approved a new facility plan which calls for significant capital improvements and bonding in the upcoming fiscal years that will increase Pleasant Hill’s debt payments to the WRA. The rate increase proposed in this ordinance is 7.25% which will increase the base rate from $8.92 to $9.57 per 1,000 gallons and the sewer access charge from $14.96 to $16.05 per unit. That increase will impact the average residential customer by $1.74 per month. The increase will be .65 cents per 1,000 gallons of water consumed, plus an increase of $1.09 per month for the sewer access charge. The last rate increase the City approved was July 2025. This increase would become effective July 1, 2026. The ordinance approval is being requested at this time to provide sufficient time for the Des Moines Water Works to make the necessary changes to their billing program prior to the July 1 effective date. Previously, the ordinance also established a new rate category for flow to be received from the City of Altoona in the future under the terms of an agreement recently approved by both Cities. The agreement provides for a combined rate that would be adjusted at the same rates as other Pleasant Hill customers on July 1, 2026 from $12.47 to $13.38 per 1,000 gallons of flowPassed 5–0 · $16.05 · Minutes · Full page →

      • Vote 5b. Third Reading of Ordinance #970 - An Ordinance Amending Chapter 106 Solid Waste Collection Fees Birch/ Motion: to approve Third Reading of Ordinance #970 - An Ordinance Amending Chapter 106 Solid Waste Collection Fees. City Manager Ben Champ explained that during the preparation of the FY27 budget there was discussion about the need to increase the solid waste collection fees and the recycling fees to reflect increases set by the Metro Waste Authority Board (MWA). The MWA manages the private hauling contractor that operates in Pleasant Hill and the current contract provides for cost adjustments each year based on tipping fees and fuel costs. The MWA Board has also made a decision to no longer subsidize the recycling operations from other landfill revenues. The two ordinances proposed will increase fees for the solid waste collection and the recycling collection fees, to be effective July 1, 2026. Approving the two ordinances at this time will provide time for the City to pass along the new rates to Des Moines Water Works so they have sufficient time to change the billing amount for July 1. The proposed increase in the solid waste fees is 2.7% to the hauler fee, and 2.5% for operational support, which increases the 96-gallon garbage container from $12.52 per month to $13.17 per month and the 48-gallon garbage container from $11.59 per month to $12.24 per month, for a total of $.65 cent increase per month. The last increase in these rates was July 1, 2025. The proposed increase for the recycling fee is 3.1% to the hauler fee, and 2.5% for operational support, increasing the rate from $4.91 per month to $5.18 per month, for a total of $.27 cent increase per month. The last increase in the recycling fees was July 1, 2025Passed 5–0 · $13.17 · Minutes · Full page →

      • Vote 9f. Resolution #060926-05 – Approve Application to Polk County Community Development Grant Hodges/ Motion: to approve Resolution #060926-05 – Approve Application to Polk County Community Development Grant. Parks and Recreation Manager Ryan Merritt explained The City of Pleasant Hill is pursuing a community development grant through Polk County Community Grants. The Community Development Grant is a funding opportunity for local service organizations, funding projects between $15,000 and $100,000. The Pleasant Hill Parks & Recreation Department is seeking to apply for a Community Development Grant in the amount of $80,000 to fund a Regional Sports Facility Feasibility Study. If awarded the grant, the funds would retain a qualified consultant to evaluate indoor field and court facility options including indoor turf and court space, support areas, and parking that would expand year-round recreational access for youth, school-aged, and adult participants in Pleasant Hill and the broader Eastern Polk County region. The study will include community engagement, stakeholder interviews, demand analysis, benchmarking of comparable facilities, preliminary cost estimates, operating projections, and potential funding strategies. The Southeast Polk Community School District has expressed interest in partnering as a key stakeholder. Deliverables will include a final written feasibility report and public presentation to guide City Council decision-making on any future capital investmentPassed 5–0 · $100,000 · Minutes · Full page →

      • Vote 11h. Resolution #060926-06 – Approve Application to Staffing for Adequate Fire and Emergency Response (SAFER) Grant Application DeMoss/ Motion: to approve Resolution #060926-06 – Approve Application to Staffing for Adequate Fire and Emergency Response (SAFER) Grant Application, with the correction to three fulltime firefighters. Fire Chief Jamie Lee explained The Department of Homeland Security (DHS) Federal Emergency Management Agency’s (FEMA) Grant Programs Directorate implements and administers the Staffing for Adequate Fire and Emergency Response (SAFER) Grants. SAFER grants provide financial assistance to help fire departments increase or maintain the number of trained, “front line” firefighters available in their communities. The goal of the SAFER program is to enhance the local fire departments’ abilities to comply with staffing, response and operational standards established by the NFPA. The Grant Application is due June 22nd, 2026. The award notification for the grant is anticipated in September 2026. If awarded, the grant would pay for salaries and benefits for 3 full-time firefighters to be hired from within or outside the department. The City is required to accept or reject the grant within 30 days of notification. When the grant is accepted, the 180-day recruitment period will start. After the recruitment, the Period of Performance will start and last for 36 months. The SAFER grant will help fund the required firefighters needed to have a fire apparatus in the station 24 hours a day 7 days a week. The grant will fund 75% per firefighter to support salary and benefits for two years and 35% for the third year. If awarded, the city would be responsible for 100% of the salary and benefit cost in year fourPassed 5–0 · Minutes · Full page →
